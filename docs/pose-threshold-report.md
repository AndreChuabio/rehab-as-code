## Interpretation

**The deeper finding is a convention mismatch, not just two bad numbers.**
pose.js targets are JOINT ANGLES (180 = straight leg; mini_squat target 135
means "bend until the knee angle reaches 135 degrees" - about 45 degrees of
flexion). The protocol library's `ROM_target_deg` reads as FLEXION FROM
NEUTRAL (0 = straight; post-ACL week 4 prescribes 60 degrees of flexion,
which is a 120-degree joint angle). The two "CONFLICT" rows are therefore
not directly comparable as printed, and neither are the "consistent" ones -
heel_slides matching at 100 is likely a coincidence of conventions rather
than agreement.

Per-row reading:

- `mini_squat` - pose coaches to ~45 deg flexion; week-4 prescribes 60 deg.
  Directionally different by ~15 deg of flexion for a post-ACL knee. Needs a
  clinical eye (Nikki) during the wiring follow-up.
- `quad_sets` - pose target 0 (min-mode: reward full extension) vs a
  protocol entry carrying ROM_target_deg 90. A quad set is an isometric at
  or near extension; the 90 in the protocol file likely describes the week's
  overall knee ROM goal, not the exercise's angle. Flags that
  `ROM_target_deg` may not be a per-exercise coaching target at all in some
  entries - the wiring follow-up must decide what the field MEANS per entry
  before consuming it.
- `no-protocol-entry` rows - either the exercise appears in no protocol YAML
  (rise/hold/presence families have no angle targets, correctly), or naming
  drifts between the pose registry and protocol files. None block anything
  today.

## Actions for the wire-prescribed-ROM follow-up

1. Define one angle convention (suggest: flexion-from-neutral, matching
   clinical language) and convert pose.js targets to it explicitly.
2. Decide per protocol entry whether ROM_target_deg is a coaching target or
   a weekly ROM goal; only the former may drive the camera.
3. mini_squat and quad_sets need clinical sign-off on the reconciled values.
